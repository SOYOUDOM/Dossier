How Resolv's assistant thinks, checks and learns

The AI model is clever, but it knows nothing about your work, forgets everything after each question, and sometimes guesses. Resolv does not try to change the model. It prepares the right papers for each question, checks the answer before you see it, and keeps a notebook for next time.

runs on your PC: instant, private runs in your Power Automate flow: where the AI thinks

The AI model is like a brilliant new colleague

…on their first day, every day

  1. Knows nothing about your work. Not your records, systems, runbooks or standards.
  2. Forgets everything after each question. Every question is day one again.
  3. Sometimes answers wrongly, with confidence. When it does not know, it may fill the gap.

So Resolv acts like a good team lead

Three jobs, around every question

  1. Prepare a small folder with only the papers this question needs.
  2. Check the work before you see it: shape, actions, and every figure against the documents.
  3. Keep a notebook of what it learns, and put the right pages in the next folder.

The whole flow

The journey of one question, in eight steps

Follow the arrows. Teal steps happen on your PC in milliseconds. The amber step is your flow in Power Automate, where the AI reads and writes. Almost all of the waiting happens there.

  1. STEP 1

    Answer here?

    Can Resolv answer by itself? Greetings, counts like "what's overdue?", "what's new?".

    Yes: answered at once, with no AI and no waiting.

    your PCmilliseconds
  2. STEP 2

    Gather

    Choose only what this question needs: the best records, notes, runbooks, past fixes, and the matching passages of your documents.

    your PC2–6 ms
  3. STEP 3

    Pack

    Fill in the blanks of one text file, the prompt: the rules, worked examples, your question and what was gathered.

    your PC1–2 ms
  4. STEP 4

    Send through one door

    The app itself cannot reach the internet. One small page, the relay, can reach exactly one address: your flow.

    your PCyour flow
  5. STEP 5

    Think

    The AI model reads everything and writes a reply in a fixed shape (JSON): the answer, its sources, any actions.

    your flowusually 5–10 s
  6. STEP 6

    Check

    Right shape? Only allowed actions? Every quote really in the document? Every figure in a passage it cites? A bad line is taken out.

    your PCmilliseconds
  7. STEP 7

    Show, and ask

    The answer, with Source, Evidence and Confidence. Any change to your records waits for your "yes", one at a time.

    your PCat once
  8. STEP 8

    Learn

    Thumbs up or down, lessons, notes, "How was it fixed?", the daily look back. Kept in your workspace for the next question.

    your PCfor next time

Then the next question starts at step 1 again, with a better notebook.

Where the time goes, drawn to scale

the flow and the AI model: a typical 7 s
your PC, all steps together: about 0.01 s (the thin teal line) the flow: nearly all of the wait

That is why the biggest speed wins come from sending less, and from not calling the AI at all when it is not needed.

A real example

Follow one question from start to finish

It was typed with a spelling mistake, exactly like this. The workspace had a 17-page password standard (a PDF) in Library → Sources.

"what is the stardard password should be?"Typed by a real user. The typo is kept on purpose: real questions have typos.

  1. 1

    Answer here? No.

    It asks what a standard says, so the AI is needed.

  2. 2

    Gather: 1 document searched, 9 passages chosen

    "stardard" was read as "standard". The passages that set rules came first, among them the user-account rule (section CSP-PWD-02, pages 8 and 9) and the local-administrator rule (CSP-PWD-04, page 10). The same header printed on every page (VERSION, DATE, CLASSIFICATION) was left out.

  3. 3

    Pack

    The passages went into the {sources} blank of the prompt, labelled S1 to S9, each with its page, lines and section.

  4. 4–5

    Send and think

    The flow ran the AI model. Suppose it replied like the answer that caused the trouble: the right rule, citing S4 and S5, plus two extra lines.

  5. 6

    Check: 2 lines taken out

    One extra line said administrators change their password "every 30 days" but cited no passage for it. The other said "every 90 days", which no passage says anywhere. Both lines are taken out. The rest is supported.

  6. 7

    Show

    The user-account answer, with its Source and Evidence, Confidence Medium, and a note that 2 lines were taken out. "Figure seen in" links to page 10, where the 30 days really is.

  7. 8

    Learn

    If the answer had been wrong, a thumbs down with the right line would be kept as a correction and as a check to re-run later.

Before version 5.9.2, lines like those made Resolv hide the whole answer and say "the documents do not specify this", when they did. The fix: take out only the line, and say so.

Step 2 in detail

What goes into the folder

The model reads every word it is sent before it starts to answer. Too much, and every answer is slow. The wrong things, and the answer is wrong. So Resolv chooses on your PC, and puts a limit on everything that grows.

60recordsthe ones that best match the question, plus totals of all of them, so "how many?" stays right
10notes you taught itthe ones that match, in full; the rest by title only
30lessons about youthe newest, plus the corrections that match this question
3runbooksthe ones that match, in full; the rest as a short index
3past fixesclosed records most like this question, with how each was fixed
3system profilesfor the systems in play
14,000characters of documentsthe best passages from Library → Sources, and their neighbours
8recent messageseach cut to 1,200 characters, plus a short running summary
30days of incidentsa summary of the recent incident history
62allowed actionsthe only things it may ask Resolv to do; 41 of them change something and always ask you first

Also in the folder: today's date, the next working day and holidays (dates are where AI most often goes wrong), the record you have open, and any file you attached. Resolv shows you the exact bytes before they go: Menu → Setup → Ask through Power Automate → Preview.

How the right page of a document is found

Index cards, and a very fast librarian

A document is read once, when you add it. Every question after that is a search over small cards, on your PC, in about 2 milliseconds. No AI chooses the cards: a chooser would have to read everything first.

Step 6 in detail

Nothing the AI sends is trusted until it is checked

A figure is a number that makes a rule: a time in days or hours, a percentage, a priority or severity, a score. Every figure in an answer about your documents must be written in a passage the answer cites.

What the AI replied (an example of the kind of reply that caused trouble)

✓ supported by S4For a user account: a passphrase of at least 17 characters, three or more unrelated words joined by hyphens.
✓ supported by S5If a passphrase is not possible: a password of at least 10 characters with three kinds of character.
✗ taken out: cites no passage with this figureFor local administrators the password is changed every 30 days.
✗ taken out: in no passage at allPasswords should also be changed every 90 days.

What you see (opened: it starts as one short line)

For a user account the standard asks for a passphrase of at least 17 characters: three or more unrelated words joined by hyphens. If a passphrase is not possible, a password of at least 10 characters with three kinds of character is allowed.

Everything the check looks at, in order

  1. The shape. The reply must be JSON. A short plain-text reply is shown as plain text; anything else unusable is dropped, with a reason.
  2. The actions. Only the 62 in Resolv's own list, each with the right details. Anything else is refused by name.
  3. A failed flow. If the flow's safety net answered instead of the AI, Resolv asks once more with only the essentials.
  4. More records. The AI may ask for more records (needRecords). Resolv finds them on your PC and asks once more.
  5. The documents. Every quote must be in its passage, word for word. A line with an unsupported figure is taken out. Only if nothing useful is left is the whole reply held back, and then Resolv says so honestly and shows what the passages do say.

Step 8 in detail

How it learns, when the model never changes

People imagine an AI learning by changing its brain. That does not happen here, and it does not need to. What changes is the notebook in your workspace, and which pages of it go into the next folder.

Your reactions go into the notebook; "Gather" takes pages from it.
The model is the same every day. The notebook grows.

👎 with the right answerKept as a correction that travels with similar questions, and as a check you can re-run: PASS or FAIL.
LessonsOne line each: your style, a preference, a habit, a person, a system, a gap. The 30 newest travel.
Notes (remember)The method in your own words. The 10 that match a question travel in full.
"How was it fixed?"One line when you close a record. Later: "This looks like D-0142, that was the SSO cache."
The daily look backAt noon, the day's work becomes lessons, notes and draft runbooks, plus one question for you.
Interview me / Learn from a documentDraft runbooks from you or from a guideline, checked against the document.
You can read it all. Every lesson and note is in Setup, in plain words.
You can undo it. Delete a lesson and it is gone from the next question.
Drafts, not decisions. An AI-written runbook stays a draft until a person approves it.
It survives a new model. Nothing learned is inside the model, so nothing is lost.

Performance and stability

Ten rules that keep it fast and steady

On one real workspace of 1,000 records, each question used to send about 164 KB. After ranking on the PC it sends about 52 KB, and it no longer grows the more you use the app.

Answer on the PC when you can

No network, no AI, no wait.

Greetings, counts, "what's new", "which source"

Choose the data with code, not a second AI call

A chooser AI must read everything first: two slow calls instead of one.

5,000 records ranked in about 6 ms; 150 documents searched in about 2 ms

Send the best, count the rest

The model needs the relevant rows. Totals keep "how many?" right.

60 records, plus totals of all

Put a limit on everything that grows

Notes, lessons and history grow every day. Without limits every question gets slower.

10 notes · 30 lessons · 8 messages · 14,000 characters

A fast model for chat, a strong one for hard jobs

The strong model is slower and costs more. Use it where it earns it.

Daily look back, learning a guideline, Diagnose, Think harder

At most one extra round trip

Every extra call is another full wait.

needRecords once; the safety-net retry once

Always set a time limit, and fall back

A silent app is worse than a simple answer.

30 s for chat, about 2 min for hard jobs; the local assistant answers if the flow fails

Never trust the reply: check it

A model can send the wrong shape, or an action that does not exist.

The validator; only the 62 known actions

Never let it change anything by itself

A confident mistake must not become a real change.

Every change waits for your yes

Measure, and show what you measured

"It feels slow" cannot be fixed. A number can.

Time under each answer · Preview of the bytes · the Sources log · tests

Steady means the same question gets the same kind of answer: the rules live in one file with checked examples, the reply has a fixed shape, search is plain arithmetic on words (not another AI), and tests run the real app with a stand-in AI that gives scripted replies.

Reuse the method

A blueprint for your own AI assistant

The same nine parts work for a helpdesk bot, an HR policy bot or a sales assistant, with any AI model. Build the checker before the prompt: it protects you from every later mistake.

  1. Front doorAnswer what needs no AI, with plain code.
  2. RetrieverYour data → a small, relevant folder for this question, chosen on your own machine.
  3. Prompt templateOne text file: rules, worked examples, blanks. Kept in version control.
  4. GatewayThe only way out: one address, time limits, a fallback.
  5. ModelAny provider. Fast for chat, strong for hard jobs. Replies in a fixed JSON shape.
  6. CheckerTrust nothing: shape, allowed actions, facts against the sources.
  7. Human in the loopReading happens at once; every change waits for a yes.
  8. Feedback and logRatings, corrections, and a log with no private text. Feeds the notebook.
  9. NotebookLessons, notes, corrections and tests, readable and deletable. Feeds the retriever.

Lessons from Resolv's history

MistakeWhat happensDo this instead
Sending everything with every questionAnswers got slower the more the app was usedRank on the PC; send the best, count the rest
Using an AI to choose what to sendThe chooser had to read everything first: two slow callsChoose with plain code, in milliseconds
A document sent with one question onlyThe next question had a summary only, and the model filled the gap with "4 hours" from somewhere elseKeep documents in a searchable store and search them for every question
Hiding the whole answer for one bad lineA correct answer became "not specified"Take out only the bad line, and say so
Reading page headers as headingsCitations named "CLASSIFICATION : OFFICIAL" as the sectionLeave out lines repeated at the same place on most pages
Orders to the AI written inside the dataMicrosoft's content filter blocked the whole questionKeep data as facts; keep orders in the prompt
Letting the AI change records directlyOne confident mistake changes real data (Resolv never allowed it)Every change waits for a yes

Plain English

Words used on this page

AI model (LLM)
The program that reads text and writes text. It remembers nothing between questions.
Prompt
Everything the model is given for one question: rules, examples, the folder and the question. Built from flow/prompt.txt.
Flow
Your Power Automate flow. It runs the model and sends the reply back.
Relay
The small page that is the only door to the network (flow/relay.html).
Token
A piece of a word. Models count what they read in tokens: more tokens, longer wait.
JSON
A strict text format for data, like {"say":"hello"}, so a reply can be checked.
Action
Something the AI may ask Resolv to do. 62 exist; the 41 that change data always ask first.
Passage
A small piece of a document, about 1,100 characters, with its page, lines and section.
Citation
Where an answer came from: [Source: document, version, page, lines, section].
Figure
A number that makes a rule: days or hours, a percentage, a priority or severity, a score.
Taken out / held back
A line removed for an unsupported figure, or a whole reply not shown because it rested on one.
Tier
Which model a question goes to: fast for everyday chat, deep for hard jobs.
Lesson
One line Resolv keeps about you or your work, sent with later questions.
Stand-in model
A fake AI used in tests. It gives scripted replies, so tests repeat exactly.
